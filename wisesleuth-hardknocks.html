Hard knocks. You have been through it. Now it is worth something.

A layoff at fifty-eight. A business that went under. A divorce that took the house. A scam that took the savings. A diagnosis. People who have been through hard knocks learn to read the fine print, to doubt the story, to follow the money — because they had to. Wise Sleuth is a place where that pays.

This is great for people who have gone through hard knocks. You know what a bad contract looks like because you signed one. You know how a company hides a loss because you watched it happen. You know a scam by its smell. Somebody out there needs exactly that, and will pay for your opinion on their scenario — never advice, always your own read of the record, under your own name.

The club is company too: meetups, library days, a walk and a coffee, each with a small price on the door so the people who come mean it. Nobody sits alone with it here.

Always remote. All digital. The work is done from wherever you are, on the record: filings, deeds, court files, archives, the open web. Nobody goes anywhere. The alternative to a private detective, without the trench coat or the retainer.
An opinion, never advice. A sleuth tells you what the record shows and what they make of it, under their own name. It is not legal, financial or investment advice, and it never claims to be.
For a fee. Nothing is free. The person asking describes the scenario and names a price; a sleuth bids; the money is held and paid out by bank transfer when the work is delivered. Free has no value here.
