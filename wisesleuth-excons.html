For ex-convicts. Use your mind. Get paid for it.

This is said here expressly, because nobody else says it: Wise Sleuth is an opportunity for people who have done time to use their minds — to take a scenario somebody describes, dig through the record, work out what it shows, and give an opinion on it, for a fee, under your own name.

You can figure things out. That is the skill, and it is the whole job. The kid who kited checks in Catch Me If You Can had a mind worth something, and in the end the FBI used it. Nobody here is judged on the past. Everybody is judged on the work — and the work builds a record of your own, on a profile with your name on it, that no one can take down.

A company’s filings that do not add up. A deed with a name on it that should not be there. A story online that somebody wants checked. A contract with a clause nobody read. People bring these scenarios to the club and name what they will pay. You read, you follow it, you write what you found and what you make of it. That is a research gig, and it pays by the job.

Always remote. All digital. The work is done from wherever you are, on the record: filings, deeds, court files, archives, the open web. Nobody goes anywhere. The alternative to a private detective, without the trench coat or the retainer.
An opinion, never advice. A sleuth tells you what the record shows and what they make of it, under their own name. It is not legal, financial or investment advice, and it never claims to be.
For a fee. Nothing is free. The person asking describes the scenario and names a price; a sleuth bids; the money is held and paid out by bank transfer when the work is delivered. Free has no value here.
